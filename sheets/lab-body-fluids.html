Cheat Sheet

Body Fluids

CSF, serous, synovial, semen, amniotic fluid, feces and BAL: tubes, appearance, counts, chemistry, crystals, patterns and safety

Checked against current sources November 2026

Cerebrospinal fluid: collection and normal values

  • Collected by lumbar puncture (also shunts and reservoirs) into numbered sterile tubes. A common three-tube protocol: tube 1 chemistry and immunology, tube 2 microbiology, tube 3 cell count. With four tubes, tube 1 is often held in reserve and tube 4 gets the cell count. Principle: the last tube has the least blood and debris from the puncture, so it gives the truest count. Follow your laboratory's protocol.
  • Count cells promptly: in one study of drain CSF, manual counts had already fallen measurably by 1.5-2.5 hours.
  • Draw a blood glucose with the tap so the CSF:blood glucose ratio can be calculated.
  • Opening pressure: 60-250 mm H2O from age 8 (10-100 mm H2O in younger children).

Traumatic tap or hemorrhage?

FindingTraumatic tapSubarachnoid hemorrhage
RBC count tube 1 to last tubeFalls from first to last tubeStays about the same in every tube
Supernatant after centrifugingClear (unless more than about 100,000 RBC/µL)Xanthochromic (pink-orange to yellow) once the bleed is hours old
TimingAt the moment of the tapXanthochromia appears over hours, is present in about 90% by 12 hours and lasts up to about 2 weeks
  • Other causes of xanthochromia: serum bilirubin of about 10-15 mg/dL (171-257 µmol/L) or more (common in newborns), CSF protein of 150 mg/dL (1.5 g/L) or more, more than 100,000 RBC/µL, and heavy carotenoid intake.
  • Correcting the WBC for added blood: predicted WBC = CSF RBC × (blood WBC ÷ blood RBC); subtract it from the observed CSF WBC. Rule of thumb: about 1 WBC per 500-1,000 RBC/µL when the blood count is normal.
  • Correcting protein: subtract about 1 mg/dL for every 1,000 RBC/µL, using the same tube for protein and cell count.
Exam tip Exam pattern: equal RBC counts in tubes 1 to 4 plus a yellow supernatant means bleeding into the CSF before the tap. A count that falls tube by tube with a clear supernatant means the needle caused it.

Safety Crystals do not rule out infection. Gout and septic arthritis can coexist, so a fluid with urate crystals and 88,000 WBC/µL still needs Gram stain (insensitive) and culture before infection is excluded.
  • Tubes: liquid EDTA for cell count and crystals; plain for chemistry; sterile for culture. Avoid oxalate, lithium heparin and powdered EDTA: they form crystal-like artifacts.
  • Keep at room temperature; refrigeration can precipitate crystals in vitro. Examine as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours.
  • Very viscous fluid: pretreat with hyaluronidase before counting (correct for the dilution). The mucin clot (Ropes) test is obsolete.

  • Physiology: seminal vesicles give about 70% of the volume (alkaline, fructose-rich), prostate about 25% (acidic). Low volume + acid pH + no fructose + no sperm suggests ejaculatory duct obstruction or absent seminal vesicles.

Safety and analyzers

  • OSHA counts CSF, synovial, pleural, pericardial, peritoneal and amniotic fluid, semen and vaginal secretions as other potentially infectious materials, so the bloodborne pathogens rules (engineering controls, PPE at no cost, hepatitis B vaccine, post-exposure follow-up) apply to every body fluid. Biosafety levels and cabinets are on the Laboratory Safety and Biosafety page.
  • Automated body fluid modes are preferred for serous and synovial counts, but check each analyzer's limit of quantitation: some cannot count reliably near the CSF limit of 5/µL. Reflex to a hemocytometer count and a stained cytospin when counts are low, flags appear, or malignant cells are possible.
  • Report the fluid type with every result, and state when an assay has not been validated for that fluid.

In depth

The summary above covers the essentials. These sections go further.

CSF: spectrophotometry for xanthochromia

Red cells that enter CSF release oxyhemoglobin within a few hours, and bilirubin forms over about 12 hours. Oxyhemoglobin alone is usually formed in vitro (for example after a traumatic tap), so bilirubin is the marker of a true bleed. Visual inspection is unreliable. The UK NEQAS approach scans centrifuged CSF from 350 to 600 nm, draws a baseline and measures net oxyhemoglobin absorbance at its 413-415 nm peak and net bilirubin absorbance at 476 nm (away from the oxyhemoglobin peak; bilirubin's own maximum is about 460 nm).

  • Tap no sooner than 12 hours after symptom onset when the question is subarachnoid hemorrhage.
  • Send the last tube collected, protect it from light (bilirubin breaks down in daylight) and store it dark at 4 °C until scanned.
  • Net bilirubin absorbance above 0.007 AU indicates a clinically significant bleed.
  • Interference: CSF protein above 1 g/L (100 mg/dL) and serum bilirubin of 20 µmol/L (1.2 mg/dL) or more raise CSF bilirubin without a bleed; the scheme adjusts for these. Large oxyhemoglobin peaks (0.1 AU or more) can hide bilirubin.

CSF: counting, worked corrections and proteins

Hemocytometer: cells/µL = cells counted × dilution factor ÷ (area counted in mm² × 0.1 mm depth). Example: undiluted CSF, all 9 large squares on both sides (18 mm², 1.8 µL) contain 36 WBC: 36 × 1 ÷ 1.8 = 20 WBC/µL (20 × 10⁶/L). Samples with many red cells are diluted with a red cell lysing diluent such as Turk solution for the WBC count, and the result is multiplied back by the dilution. The Manual Cell Count calculator does this arithmetic.

Corrections raise specificity but miss some meningitis; many clinicians act on the uncorrected count

  • Differential on a cytocentrifuged, stained smear. Normal CSF cells are lymphocytes and monocytes with rare neutrophils or eosinophils. Neutrophils point to bacterial (or very early viral) infection; lymphocytes to viral, fungal or tuberculous infection; eosinophils can appear in fungal meningitis.
  • Oligoclonal bands: isoelectric focusing on paired CSF and serum, read side by side. Bands in CSF only (or extra bands in CSF) mean intrathecal IgG synthesis, as in multiple sclerosis. Identical bands in both (mirror pattern) mean a systemic response passing into CSF. Bands are not specific for MS.
  • IgG index = (CSF IgG ÷ serum IgG) ÷ (CSF albumin ÷ serum albumin). Dividing by the albumin quotient (which itself reflects the blood-CSF barrier) corrects for IgG that leaks in from blood. Above about 0.7 (laboratory-specific) suggests intrathecal synthesis. It is less sensitive than oligoclonal bands (about 50-75% vs 90-100% in MS). Kappa free light chains are a newer marker.
  • CSF VDRL: specific but only 30-75% sensitive for neurosyphilis; blood contamination can cause false positives.
  • Lactate: above 35.1 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) supports bacterial over viral meningitis when CSF was obtained before antibiotics.

  • Chylous ascites: ascitic triglyceride over about 1.2 mmol/L (about 106 mg/dL) or ascitic:serum triglyceride over 1.
  • Bloody ascites (RBC over 10 × 10⁹/L): subtract 1 neutrophil per 250 RBC before applying the 250/µL SBP cutoff. Example: 250 neutrophils/µL with 20,000 RBC/µL → 250 − 80 = 170/µL. Up to 60% of patients with SBP-range counts have negative cultures, so the count, not the culture, drives treatment. Urine leukocyte esterase strips are not reliable for SBP.
  • Cytology finds malignancy in only about 50-60% of malignant pleural effusions. Tumor markers on fluid only after inconclusive cytology.
  • Pericardial fluid: few tests are specific; pleural cutoffs are borrowed with limited evidence. Mycobacterial culture (or ADA) supports tuberculous pericarditis; pericardial pH has no clinical value.
  • Specimens: EDTA for cell counts, heparin or plain for chemistry, an anaerobic heparinized syringe for pleural pH (never pH paper or a pH meter), paired serum within 1 hour (ascites: drawn together). Count cells within 1 hour. Large syringes are not transport containers. Reject clotted or grossly hemolyzed fluid unless the exception is documented.

Worked examples. Light's: pleural protein 3.8 g/dL with serum 6.5 g/dL gives 0.58, which alone makes the fluid an exudate. SAAG: serum albumin 2.8 g/dL minus ascitic albumin 1.2 g/dL = 1.6 g/dL (16 g/L), a portal hypertension pattern. SBP: 600 nucleated cells/µL with 50% neutrophils = 300 neutrophils/µL, above 250.

Synovial fluid at the microscope

  1. Mix well and make a wet preparation from native fluid (a cytospin concentrates crystals in a monolayer and keeps a slide for teaching).
  2. Scan at 100× with crossed polarizers, then identify at 400× or higher. Report a positive only with typical crystals in at least two per field across 10 random fields, plus shape, birefringence, intra- or extracellular location and number per high-power field.
  3. Insert the first-order red compensator and note the slow axis marked on it. Turn the stage so a crystal lies parallel to that axis: urate turns yellow, CPPD turns blue. Turn it 90°: the colors swap.
  4. Memory aid: urate is yellow when parallel; CPPD does the opposite and is weaker.
  5. Separate crystals from look-alikes: fibrin, debris, talc and anticoagulant crystals (oxalate, lithium heparin, powdered EDTA) and injected corticosteroid.
  • Glucose: compare with a simultaneous (preferably fasting) serum glucose. The serum-minus-fluid difference is under about 11 mg/dL (0.6 mmol/L) normally, above about 20 mg/dL (1.1 mmol/L) in inflammatory fluids and above about 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L) in septic fluids. Measure within 1 hour.
  • Counts: automated body fluid mode is preferred; fat, cartilage fragments and crystals can falsely raise WBC or eosinophil counts. Dilute turbid fluid with isotonic saline; lyse excess red cells with 0.3% saline. Differential on a cytocentrifuged stained smear.
  • Total protein and LD are measured but are nonspecific; lactate only when sepsis is suspected; uric acid on fluid only in special cases.
  • Less virulent organisms (gonococcal, Lyme, tuberculous, fungal) and partly treated infection give lower counts and neutrophil percentages, and the Gram stain is insensitive, so culture every joint in which infection is possible.

Semen, amniotic fluid and feces in more depth

  • Semen is examined after liquefaction and within 60 minutes of ejaculation. Mix well before every sampling. Viscous samples can be treated with an enzyme such as chymotrypsin or bromelain, not by forcing them through a needle.
  • WHO 6th edition (2021) reports the 5th centile of fertile men (about 3,500 men whose partners conceived within a year) and stresses that these values do not separate fertile from infertile men. It reinstates rapid vs slow progressive motility and moves leukocyte and antisperm antibody testing to the extended examination.
  • Post-vasectomy: about 80% of men are azoospermic by 12 weeks and nearly all the rest have rare nonmotile sperm. The number of ejaculations does not predict clearance.
Now rarely done: ACOG reserves amniocentesis for lung maturity mainly for poorly dated pregnancies when delivery is planned

  • Fecal fat interferences: laxatives, enemas, barium, mineral oil, fat substitutes, and contamination with urine or toilet water. Fat over 40 g/day suggests pancreatic insufficiency or small-bowel mucosal disease but cannot tell which.
  • Fecal elastase: watery stool dilutes it and can give a falsely low (falsely abnormal) result, so a formed specimen is preferred.
  • gFOBT reacts with any peroxidase (meat, some foods), so diet rules matter; FIT detects human hemoglobin and is the screening stool test most programs now use.

BAL handling and patterns

  • Transport fresh at room temperature if delivery is immediate, on ice (4 °C) if 30-60 minutes; beyond 1 hour centrifuge and resuspend cells in nutrient medium. Never freeze. Analyze within 1 hour in saline (2-3 hours in nutrient medium); specimens older than 24 hours are unsuitable.
  • Strain gross mucus through loose gauze or dissolve small amounts with dithiothreitol; use labware that cells do not stick to.
  • Recovery under 30% of the instilled volume can distort the differential. Epithelial cells over 5% mean a suboptimal sample.
  • Lymphocytes over 15% (over 25% suggests granulomatous disease such as sarcoidosis or hypersensitivity pneumonitis; CD4/CD8 over 4 is highly specific for sarcoidosis), neutrophils over 3%, eosinophils over 1% (25% or more is virtually diagnostic of eosinophilic pneumonia), mast cells over 0.5%. Bloody fluid that increases with each aliquot suggests alveolar hemorrhage; milky PAS-positive fluid suggests alveolar proteinosis.
