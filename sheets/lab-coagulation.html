Hemostasis and Coagulation Testing

Platelets and the cascade, factors and inhibitors, PT/INR, aPTT, TT, fibrinogen, D-dimer, mixing studies, platelet function, anti-Xa, specimen rules and the bleeding and clotting disorders

INR = (patient PT ÷ MNPT)^ISI. MNPT is the geometric mean PT of at least 20 healthy people on the same reagent; ISI is the reagent's sensitivity compared with the WHO reference thromboplastin. Example: PT 24 s, MNPT 12 s, ISI 1.2 gives 2^1.2 = 2.3. INR is built for warfarin and is not used for DOACs.

Typical intervals (each laboratory sets its own; the ASCP composite ranges list none): PT 10–13 s, aPTT 28–34 s, fibrinogen 180–400 mg/dL (1.8–4.0 g/L). The PT and aPTT do not prolong until a factor falls to about 30% of normal.

Mixing studies

  • Patient plasma is mixed 1:1 with pooled normal plasma, which guarantees at least 50% of every factor: enough to normalize the PT or aPTT if a factor is simply missing.
  • Test the mix at once and again after incubation at 37 °C (Merck: 1 hour).
  • Corrects immediately and after incubation: factor deficiency.
  • Corrects or nearly corrects at once, then prolongs after incubation: time- and temperature-dependent inhibitor, classically an anti-factor VIII antibody (acquired hemophilia or an alloantibody in hemophilia A).
  • Does not correct, immediately or after incubation: lupus anticoagulant (confirm with a phospholipid-dependent test) or heparin (check TT, reptilase time or anti-Xa).
Exam tip Exams pair the patterns: bleeding plus a non-correcting aPTT that worsens with incubation is a factor VIII inhibitor; clotting or pregnancy loss plus a non-correcting aPTT is lupus anticoagulant. LA prolongs the test tube but causes thrombosis, not bleeding.

  • Light transmission aggregometry: platelet-rich plasma (200–250 g for 10 min, no brake) clears as platelets clump; finish within 3–4 h; unreliable with platelet counts below 100 × 10³/µL (100 × 10⁹/L); stop aspirin and thienopyridines 10 days and reversible NSAIDs 3 days before, when possible.
  • PFA-100/200: whole blood under high shear through a collagen membrane with epinephrine or ADP; the result is the closure time. Long with VWD (types 2 and 3 often no closure) and severe platelet defects; the epinephrine and ADP pair helps separate these from aspirin effect and mild defects. Platelet count and hematocrit also change it, and it is insensitive to mild defects. Reject Hct below 30% or above 50%, or platelets below 100 × 10³/µL.

Specimen rules

  • 3.2% buffered sodium citrate (105–109 mmol/L), 9 parts blood to 1 part citrate. 3.8% citrate lengthens PT and aPTT against 3.2% reference ranges.
  • Fill to the mark (at least 90%). Under about 89% fill the aPTT is biased; underfilled tubes are rejected. Clotted samples are always rejected.
  • Order of draw: blood cultures, then citrate, then serum and additive tubes. A discard tube is needed only with a winged (butterfly) set, to fill the tubing's dead space.
  • Hematocrit above 55%: too little plasma for the citrate. Citrate to leave in the tube C (mL) = 0.00185 × (100 − Hct%) × blood volume (mL). Example: Hct 65%, 2.7 mL draw: C = 0.00185 × 35 × 2.7 = 0.17 mL, so remove about 0.13 mL of a 0.3 mL fill.
  • Keep capped at room temperature (15–25 °C); do not refrigerate. Centrifuge 1500 g for 15 min to platelet-poor plasma (below 10 × 10⁹/L platelets); double-centrifuge for lupus anticoagulant and for frozen plasma.
  • Time limits: PT 24 h; aPTT 4 h; aPTT for heparin monitoring centrifuged within 1 h; factors V and VIII within 3 h; platelet function within 3–4 h.
  • Freeze at −70 °C or colder if delayed (−20 °C: about 2 weeks); thaw at 37 °C and do not refreeze.
Safety Call critical results promptly to the care team (for example an INR above 5 or fibrinogen below 80 mg/dL [0.8 g/L]; each laboratory sets its own list). An underfilled or clotted tube is rejected and recollected; a line draw is noted on the report, and a long TT with a normal reptilase time points to heparin in the sample.

In depth

The summary above covers the essentials. These sections go further.

The cell-based model and the laboratory pathways

PhaseWhereWhat happens
InitiationTissue factor-bearing cell at the injuryTF/VIIa activates X and IX; Xa activates V; Xa/Va makes a small amount of thrombin
AmplificationPlatelet surfaceThat thrombin fully activates platelets and activates V, VIII and XI
PropagationActivated platelet surfaceIXa/VIIIa (tenase) makes Xa; Xa/Va (prothrombinase) makes a burst of thrombin

The extrinsic, intrinsic and common pathways describe the test tube, not the body. The PT starts with tissue factor (extrinsic), the aPTT with a contact activator such as silica or ellagic acid (intrinsic). In the body, factor XII, prekallikrein and HMWK are not needed: their deficiencies make a very long aPTT with no bleeding. Hemophilia bleeds because tenase (IXa/VIIIa) on platelets drives the thrombin burst.

  • Vitamin K-dependent: II, VII, IX, X, protein C and protein S. Warfarin blocks the recycling of vitamin K, so these fall; VII falls first.
  • Factor VIII is not made by hepatocytes and rises as an acute-phase protein: it stays normal or high in liver disease but is consumed in DIC.
  • Factor XIII deficiency (delayed bleeding, poor wound healing) needs a specific FXIII assay; a clot that dissolves in 5 M urea suggests activity below about 3–5%.

D-dimer is made only when plasmin digests fibrin that factor XIII has cross-linked, so it shows that thrombin formed in the body and the clot is being lysed. Report the unit and method: FEU results are about twice DDU results (1.0 mg/L FEU ≈ 0.5 mg/L DDU). D-dimer runs on citrated plasma.

Factor assays, inhibitors and VWD testing

  • Factor assays report activity as a percentage of normal (or IU/dL). A long aPTT with a normal PT calls for VIII, IX and XI; a long PT alone calls for VII.
  • Hemophilia severity: mild 5–49%, moderate 1–5%, severe below 1%. Every new hemophilia A diagnosis also gets VWF antigen, activity and multimers, because VWD (especially type 2N) can lower factor VIII.
  • Inhibitors develop in about 30% of patients with severe hemophilia A and about 3% with hemophilia B; autoantibodies to factor VIII also arise postpartum and in older adults (acquired hemophilia).
  • Bethesda assay: serial dilutions of patient plasma incubated with normal plasma; the residual factor VIII gives the titer in Bethesda units. The Nijmegen modification buffers the normal plasma and uses factor VIII-deficient plasma or buffered albumin as the diluent; the modified Nijmegen-Bethesda assay adds heat inactivation first to remove residual factor VIII. Low titer is below 5 BU/mL, high titer 5 BU/mL or more.

Exam tip Ratio cutoff: the ASH/ISTH/NHF/WFH 2021 guideline confirms type 2 (2A, 2B, 2M) at an activity/antigen ratio below 0.7; older tables use 0.6. VWF results move: group O people run 25–35% lower, and stress, exercise, pregnancy, inflammation and estrogen raise VWF and can hide type 1. A normal result in a patient with a strong bleeding history may need repeating.

Lupus anticoagulant and antiphospholipid testing

  • Use two tests based on different principles: a dilute Russell viper venom time (dRVVT) and an LA-sensitive aPTT (or the related silica clotting time).
  • Three steps: screen with a low-phospholipid reagent, mix with pooled normal plasma, and confirm with a high-phospholipid reagent that shortens the time (phospholipid dependence). Results are usually given as normalized screen/confirm ratios against cutoffs set on at least 120 normal samples.
  • Double-centrifuged platelet-poor plasma, at least 1 mL.
  • Avoid testing during acute thrombosis or pregnancy. On DOACs do not test (or test at least 48 h after the last dose, or after a DOAC removal agent); most dRVVT reagents now contain a heparin neutralizer that covers therapeutic heparin levels; on warfarin, a 1:1 mix can be considered when the INR is 1.5 to below 3.0.
  • APS needs at least one clinical criterion plus a laboratory criterion (LA, anticardiolipin or anti-beta2-glycoprotein I) persisting at least 12 weeks. Triple positivity carries the highest risk.

  • Anti-Xa principle: the drug (heparins through antithrombin, rivaroxaban and apixaban directly) inhibits a known amount of added factor Xa; the residual Xa cleaves a chromogenic substrate, so the color is inversely proportional to the drug level. Assays that add antithrombin can overestimate heparin in AT deficiency; in cirrhosis anti-Xa underestimates the drug.
  • Heparin contamination or neutralization: a long TT with a normal reptilase time means heparin. ROTEM HEPTEM uses heparinase to remove heparin and compare with INTEM.

ROTEM assays: EXTEM (tissue factor), INTEM (ellagic acid), FIBTEM (EXTEM with cytochalasin D to block platelets, so the clot is fibrin only), APTEM (EXTEM with an antifibrinolytic), HEPTEM (INTEM with heparinase). Disorders in more depth

DisorderLaboratory detail
DICCauses: obstetric (abruption, amniotic fluid embolism, retained dead fetus), gram-negative sepsis, cancer and APL, shock, trauma, ABO-incompatible transfusion. Acute: steep platelet fall, long PT/aPTT, rapidly falling fibrinogen, high D-dimer, schistocytes. Chronic (cancer, aneurysm): mild changes, aPTT can be short; a falling fibrinogen on two draws matters because fibrinogen is acute-phase.
HITAntibodies to heparin bound to platelet factor 4 (PF4). Four Ts: thrombocytopenia size, timing, thrombosis, other causes. Score 0–3 low (no testing recommended), 4–5 intermediate, 6–8 high. Immunoassay (ELISA, higher OD = more likely), then functional assay: SRA positive at 20% or more serotonin release with low-dose heparin that falls by half or more with high-dose heparin (100 IU/mL); or HIPA. Rapid onset within hours if heparin was given in the past 30 days. UFH up to 2.4% of patients; LMWH about 10 times less immunogenic but cross-reacts.
TTPADAMTS13 activity usually below 10% before TTP appears. Acquired cases have an anti-ADAMTS13 autoantibody; congenital (Upshaw-Schulman) is autosomal recessive. Microthrombi lack fibrin and red cells, unlike DIC. HUS has more kidney failure.

Add the four domains (0–8): 0–3 low probability (under 5%), 4–5 intermediate, 6–8 high. Worked example: platelets 250 to 100 × 10⁹/L (60% fall, nadir 100) = 2; fall on day 7 of first heparin = 2; new DVT = 2; sepsis also possible = 1; total 7, high probability, so the immunoassay is sent and heparin is stopped.

Safety Suspected HIT is urgent: when the 4Ts score is intermediate or high, the care team stops all heparin (including line flushes) and starts a non-heparin anticoagulant while results are pending. A falling platelet count on heparin should be reported, not just flagged.
