The exam at a glance

FeatureDetail
Items100 multiple choice, four options (A to D), one best answer; includes unscored field-test items (number not published)
Time2 hours 30 minutes, about 90 seconds per item
FormatComputer adaptive at a fixed length: every candidate answers all 100
NavigationNo skipping; flag items and review or change answers if time remains after the last item
CalculatorOn-screen calculator provided
ScoringScaled score 100 to 999; 400 to pass. Based on the pattern of responses, not a percent correct
ResultsPreliminary pass or fail on screen; score report within 4 business days. Failing reports show a breakdown by area
WherePearson VUE test centers
Fee$260 application fee, non-refundable
RetakesUp to 5 attempts per eligibility route; next attempt no sooner than 30 days after the last; new application and fee each time

The adaptive engine starts near the passing standard, gives a slightly harder item after a correct answer and a slightly easier one after a miss, and aims for about a 50% chance of a correct answer on each item. Areas are mixed throughout, not given in blocks.

Exam tip Field-test items look exactly like scored ones. Give every item full effort, and do not read a run of hard items as a sign you are failing: an adaptive exam is meant to feel hard.

Items test three levels: knowledge, comprehension, and application or analysis. Regulatory questions use U.S. sources (AABB, FDA, CLIA), and donor eligibility questions follow the regulations in force as of June 2026. Results on the exam are shown in both conventional and SI units.

The app's Mock Exam is a fixed 100-item form with a 150-minute limit and the counts above. It is not adaptive and scores as a percent, not a scaled score, so treat the result as practice, not a prediction.

Ranges and calculations to learn

The guideline publishes composite reference ranges for chemistry, blood gases, hematology, CSF, semen and urine. They are not shown during the exam, and every matching value on the exam can be read against them; other ranges are given when needed. ASCP says they are for exam purposes only, not clinical use. How to study: 6 weeks

  1. Week 1: read the content guideline, take a timed Mock Exam as a baseline, then study the Reference Ranges the Exam Uses page and work each calculator in the table above.
  2. Week 2, Blood Banking: ABO, H and Rh Typing; Antibody Screen and Identification; Compatibility Testing and Blood Components; Transfusion Reactions and HDFN. Practice Blood Banking questions daily.
  3. Week 3, Hematology: Red Cell Morphology and the Anemias; CBC, Analyzers and Spurious Results; White Cells, Leukemia and Lymphoma; Hemostasis and Coagulation Testing.
  4. Week 4, Chemistry: Clinical Chemistry: Analytes and Disease Patterns; Electrolytes, Acid-Base and Blood Gases; Endocrinology, Drug Monitoring and Toxicology; Chemistry Methods and Instrumentation. Take a second Mock Exam at the end of the week.
  5. Week 5, Microbiology and Immunology: Gram Stain and Bacterial Identification; Specimens, Cultures and Susceptibility Testing; Mycobacteria, Fungi, Parasites and Viruses; Immunology and Serology. Practice Microbiology and Immunology questions daily, and read the rationale of every miss.
  6. Week 6, the smaller areas and review: Urinalysis; Body Fluids; Quality Control, Validation and Regulation; Laboratory Safety and Biosafety; Laboratory Mathematics. Practice Urinalysis and Other Body Fluids and Laboratory Operations questions. Take a final Mock Exam, then work your Missed Questions and Trouble Questions.
  7. Every day: Daily Review. Twice a week: a Weakest Subject set.
Exam tip The four large areas carry 68 to 88% of the exam between them (76 items in the app's mock), but the three small areas still hold 15 to 30% (24 items in the mock). Do not skip Laboratory Operations: QC rules, safety and lab math are quick points.

Morphology-heavy topics (smears, Gram stains, sediment, parasites) are taught here in words and tables. Pair them with a bench atlas or your program's slides so you can recognize the cells and organisms you can describe.

Test-day tips

  • Pace at about 90 seconds per item, and check the clock at items 25, 50 and 75.
  • You must answer each item before moving on. If unsure, pick your best answer, flag it, and come back if time remains.
  • Use the on-screen calculator for every multi-step calculation, and keep to one unit system: results are shown in both conventional and SI units.
  • Read for the one best answer: the three wrong options are written to be plausible.
  • Schedule directly with Pearson VUE, and reschedule at least one full business day ahead if you must.
  • Test within the eligibility period on your Admission Notification; it is not extended, and missing it means a new application and fee.

Eligibility routes for MLS(ASCP)

From the MLS credential page; check it for the current wording before you apply
RouteRequirements
1Bachelor's degree plus a NAACLS-accredited MLS program completed within the last 5 years
2Valid MLT(ASCP), a bachelor's degree with 16 semester hours of biology (one semester of microbiology) and 16 of chemistry (one semester of organic chemistry or biochemistry), and 2 years of full-time clinical experience within the last 5 years
3Discontinued January 1, 2023
4Bachelor's degree with the same science hours plus 5 years of full-time clinical experience within the last 10 years
5Valid MLS(ASCPi), a U.S.-equivalent bachelor's degree, and 5 years of experience within the last 10 years; no re-examination
6A 50-week U.S. military medical laboratory course within the last 10 years, a bachelor's degree with the science hours, and 1 year of experience
  • Experience for Routes 2, 4 and 5 must be in an acceptable laboratory (CMS CLIA, DoD CLIP, JCI or ISO 15189) and must cover blood bank, chemistry, hematology, microbiology, immunology, and urinalysis and body fluids.
  • Completing an accredited MLS or MLT program, or a foreign MLS clinical program, within 5 years can replace 1 year of the experience.
  • Students in accredited programs should apply no more than 6 weeks before they complete the program.
  • MLS(ASCPi) candidates take the same content guideline; international routes are listed on the ASCPi tab of the MLS page.

Results, retakes and renewal

  • Score review: $25, requested within 30 days. ASCP does not rescore or release items.
  • After five failed attempts under one route, a candidate must qualify under another route.
  • The credential is time-limited to 3 years. Renew through the Credential Maintenance Program (CMP): 36 points per 3-year cycle.
  • CMP points: 2 each in blood banking, chemistry, hematology and microbiology; 1 in laboratory or patient safety; 1 in medical ethics; 26 in the specialty or related areas.
  • CMP fee: $110. Reinstating an expired credential adds a $135 fee.
  • With CMP the credential is written MLS(ASCP) with a superscript CM.
  • From January 2026, new credentials come with a digital badge; wall certificates are no longer issued.
Exam tip Exam items are copyrighted, and candidates pledge not to share them. Never use or pass on recalled questions: ASCP can revoke a credential for it.
