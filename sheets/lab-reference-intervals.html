Reference Ranges the Exam Uses

The ASCP BOC composite reference ranges for chemistry, blood gases, hematology, CSF, semen and urine in conventional and SI units, and the calculations the MLS and MLT exams expect

Checked against current sources November 2026

What these ranges are

The ASCP Board of Certification publishes one set of composite reference ranges with the MLS and MLT content guidelines (revised June 9, 2026). They cover all genders and ethnic groups and come from published sources such as textbooks; the Hematology and Chemistry Examination Committees review them every year. Any laboratory value on the exam can be judged against them. The exam does not show them, so learn them; when a question needs any other range, the question gives it. Results on the exam appear in both conventional and SI units.

Safety ASCP states that these ranges are for examination purposes only and should not be used for clinical decisions. At the bench, use the reference intervals your own laboratory has established or verified.
Exam tip Because the ranges combine men and women, some are wide: hemoglobin 12.0-18.0 g/dL, hematocrit 35-50%, MCV 76-100 fL. Judge a value as high or low only when it falls outside these limits.

In depth

The summary above covers the essentials. These sections go further.

Reading conventional and SI units

Conventional units are those most US laboratories report (mass per deciliter, cells per microliter, percent). SI units use moles for substances of known molar mass, liters as the volume, and fractions in place of percent. The exam shows both, so either column of the tables above answers the question. To convert mg/dL to mmol/L, multiply by 10 (deciliters to liters) and divide by the molar mass in g/mol: glucose is 180 g/mol, so 10 ÷ 180 = 0.0555.

  • Units that do not change: sodium, potassium, chloride, total CO2 and bicarbonate are already in mmol/L; pH, specific gravity, MCV (fL) and MCH (pg) have no conversion.
  • A count per microliter becomes a count per liter by multiplying by 10⁶: so 10³/µL is 10⁹/L, and CSF cells 5/µL are 5 × 10⁶/L.
  • Semen counts per mL become counts per L by multiplying by 10³: 20 × 10⁶/mL is 20 × 10⁹/L.
  • HbA1c is reported as NGSP percent (conventional) or IFCC mmol/mol (SI); the guideline pairs < 5.7% with < 39 mmol/mol. The two scales are not related by a simple multiplier.
  • Round only at the end of a calculation; a converted value that differs from the guideline in the last digit is a rounding difference, not a different range.
Exam tip Worked example: a fasting glucose of 6.1 mmol/L is 6.1 ÷ 0.0555 = 110 mg/dL, above the 74-100 mg/dL range. A serum creatinine of 150 µmol/L is 150 ÷ 88.4 = 1.7 mg/dL, above 0.8-1.2.
