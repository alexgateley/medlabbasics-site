Exam tip Test logic: suspect deficiency, stimulate (cosyntropin); suspect excess, suppress (dexamethasone, glucose for GH, saline for aldosterone). A gland that is autonomous ignores the feedback signal.
Safety Hook effect: in a one-step sandwich immunoassay, extreme antigen (prolactin in a macroadenoma, hCG in a molar pregnancy) saturates both antibodies and the result reads falsely low or negative. Repeat on a dilution: a hook shows a much higher corrected value.

Therapeutic drug monitoring

  • Trough: drawn just before the next dose (aminoglycosides: within 30 minutes before it).
  • Peak: timing varies by drug. Gentamicin: 30 minutes after the end of a 30-minute infusion, or 60 minutes after IM.
  • Steady state: about 5 half-lives after starting or changing a dose; drawing earlier underestimates the level.
  • Digoxin: at least 6-8 hours after the dose, after distribution; earlier samples read falsely high.
  • A level is useless without the time of the last dose and the time of collection.

Exam tip A gap that the measured ethanol does not explain is a co-ingested toxic alcohol until proven otherwise. A normal gap late after ingestion does not rule one out: the parent alcohol has already become acid.

Drug screens

  • Screen: immunoassay with a cutoff; reports a class by cross-reactivity with the calibrator drug.
  • Confirm: a different principle with mass spectrometric identification (GC-MS, LC-MS, LC-MS/MS) at a lower, analyte-specific cutoff.
  • Opiate screens are built on morphine: they miss fentanyl and methadone and may miss oxycodone and buprenorphine. Order specific tests.
  • Forensic (workplace) testing adds chain of custody, a temperature check (32-38 °C within 4 minutes) and specimen validity testing.

Thyroid testing in depth

  • About 75% of bound thyroid hormone rides on thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG). TBG rises with pregnancy, estrogen and estrogen-progestin contraceptives, acute hepatitis and an inherited X-linked excess; it falls with low hepatic synthesis, anabolic steroids, nephrotic syndrome and glucocorticoid excess. Total T4 and T3 follow TBG; free T4 does not, which is why free T4 is measured.
  • Large doses of phenytoin and aspirin displace T4 from TBG and can lower total T4 spuriously.
  • Secondary hypothyroidism after pituitary surgery or with a pituitary mass gives low free T4 with a low or inappropriately normal TSH. TSH alone would miss it, so free T4 must be measured when pituitary disease is known.
  • Antibodies: thyroid peroxidase antibodies are present in almost all patients with Hashimoto thyroiditis and in most with Graves disease; TSH receptor antibody (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin) causes Graves hyperthyroidism; thyroglobulin antibodies interfere with thyroglobulin measurement, which matters when thyroglobulin is used as a cancer marker after thyroidectomy.
  • Euthyroid sick (nonthyroidal illness): low T3 with variable TSH in a sick inpatient; thyroid tests in acute illness are interpreted with caution.

Adrenal testing in depth

  1. Cushing screen: 1 mg dexamethasone at 11 PM to midnight, cortisol at 8-9 AM. Normal suppression is below 1.8 µg/dL (50 nmol/L). Late-night (midnight) serum cortisol is normally below 1.8 µg/dL; salivary samples are collected at home on 2-3 nights and their cutoffs depend on the assay. 24-h urine free cortisol above 120 µg/24 h (331 nmol/24 h) is typical of Cushing; 100-150 is common in obesity, depression and polycystic ovaries.
  2. Oral estrogen raises cortisol-binding globulin and so total serum cortisol, which can make a dexamethasone test look unsuppressed; diurnal variation stays normal.
  3. Once cortisol excess is proven, plasma ACTH separates the source: undetectable ACTH means an adrenal source; detectable or high ACTH means pituitary (Cushing disease) or ectopic ACTH. Desmopressin or CRH stimulation and inferior petrosal sinus sampling (central to peripheral ACTH ratio above 3 after desmopressin points to the pituitary) localize it. High-dose dexamethasone (more than 50% fall suggests the pituitary) is now little used.
  4. Adrenal insufficiency: low morning cortisol is below 5 µg/dL (138 nmol/L). ACTH 50 pg/mL (11 pmol/L) or more with low cortisol means primary (Addison) disease; ACTH below 5 pg/mL (1.1 pmol/L) with low cortisol means secondary. Typical chemistry: sodium below 135, potassium above 5 mmol/L, Na:K under 30, low glucose, raised BUN.
  5. Cosyntropin stimulation: 250 µg IV or IM, cortisol at 0, 30 and 60 minutes; a peak below 18 µg/dL (500 nmol/L) makes insufficiency likely. The cutoff depends on the cortisol assay: a laboratory that changes assays must give clinicians the cutoff validated for the new one.
  6. Primary aldosteronism: morning, ideally recumbent, plasma aldosterone and renin activity, off thiazides, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and beta-blockers for 4-6 weeks where possible. Aldosterone above 15 ng/dL with low renin and a ratio above 20 (ng/dL per ng/mL/h) is typical; the cutoff depends on the assay and units. Saline infusion or salt loading that fails to suppress aldosterone confirms. Both renin and aldosterone high means secondary aldosteronism.
  7. Pheochromocytoma: plasma free metanephrines (up to 99% sensitive) or 24-h urine fractionated metanephrines. Methyldopa, MAO inhibitors, some antidepressants, illicit stimulants and extreme stress raise catecholamines and metabolites. VMA (normal under 10 mg/24 h) and urine catecholamines are less accurate and rarely used now.

Pituitary, reproductive, parathyroid and vitamin D

AnalyteWhat the lab needs to know
ProlactinRaised by pregnancy, stress, sleep, nipple stimulation, primary hypothyroidism, chronic kidney disease, stalk compression and many drugs (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, opioids, estrogens). Prolactinoma is usually more than 5 times normal; a nonfunctioning mass rarely exceeds 3-4 times. A large tumor with a modest prolactin needs a diluted repeat (hook effect).
Growth hormone and IGF-1GH is pulsatile, so a random level does not diagnose. IGF-1 is stable and screens for excess (3-10 times raised in acromegaly). Normally GH falls below 1 ng/mL (1 µg/L) within 2 h of 75 g glucose; failure to suppress confirms excess.
hCGTwo-site sandwich assays. Urine tests are about 99% sensitive for pregnancy. In early normal pregnancy serum hCG doubles every 1.4-2.1 days; a slow rise or fall suggests ectopic pregnancy or loss. Very high levels in molar pregnancy (above about 500,000 mIU/mL) can give a false negative by hook effect.
TestosteroneDraw before 10 AM; confirm on a repeat. High LH and FSH with low testosterone means primary (testicular) failure; low or inappropriately normal LH and FSH means secondary. Free or bioavailable testosterone helps when SHBG is abnormal or after age 50.
Estradiol and FSHEstradiol is the main ovarian estrogen, made under FSH and LH stimulation. Primary ovarian insufficiency (before age 40): FSH high (above 20, usually above 30 mIU/mL) with estradiol low (usually under 20 pg/mL [73 pmol/L]) on two samples a month apart. As with testosterone, a high gonadotropin with a low sex steroid places the failure in the gonad.
PTH and calciumPrimary hyperparathyroidism: calcium usually under 12 mg/dL (3 mmol/L), phosphate low, PTH inappropriately high. Malignancy (PTHrP): calcium often over 12, PTH suppressed. Renal secondary hyperparathyroidism: phosphate high, PTH high. Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia: PTH normal or high but low urine calcium, with a calcium-to-creatinine clearance ratio under 0.01 (1%; 1-4% in primary hyperparathyroidism).
25-hydroxyvitamin DReflects body stores and is the test for vitamin D status (D2 plus D3). Deficiency: phosphate low, alkaline phosphatase high, PTH normal or high, calcium low or normal. 1,25-dihydroxy D is measured only when the diagnosis is unclear; it is usually undetectable in severe deficiency.

Drug monitoring in depth

  • Half-life is the time for the concentration to fall by half. On regular dosing, levels climb for about 5 half-lives before reaching steady state, which is why toxicity can develop slowly in older adults with reduced renal clearance.
  • Aminoglycosides: peak and trough after the second or third dose. Once-daily gentamicin aims for a high peak (15-20 µg/mL [31-42 µmol/L], 16-24 in critical illness) and a trough under 1 µg/mL at 18-24 h. Creatinine every 2-3 days; long courses need audiometry. Renal toxicity is often reversible, ototoxicity often not.
  • Vancomycin: for serious MRSA infection, dose to an AUC of 400-600 using two levels (1-2 h after the end of the infusion and a trough) or Bayesian software with one or two levels; continuous infusion targets 17-25 µg/mL at steady state.
  • Digoxin: mainly renally cleared. Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia and hypercalcemia sensitize the heart, so toxicity can occur below 2.0 ng/mL. Check potassium and magnesium with the level. LC-MS/MS is the reference method when immunoassay cross-reactivity is suspected.
  • Phenytoin: total 10-20 µg/mL, free 1-2 µg/mL, about 10% free. Hypoalbuminemia, renal or hepatic impairment, uremia and competing highly bound drugs raise the free fraction, so a 'therapeutic' total can hide a toxic free level (nystagmus, ataxia). Correction equations (Winter-Tozer, often called Sheiner-Tozer) misclassify many critically ill patients; measure free phenytoin directly when available. Hepatic clearance is nonlinear, so levels can change sharply over time.
  • Lithium: steady state in about 5-7 days; draw 12 ± 1 h after the last dose (a sample drawn soon after a dose reads high). Toxicity bands: about 1.5-2.5 mmol/L nausea, tremor, ataxia; 2.5-3.5 confusion and ECG changes; above 3.5 seizures and coma. Use a sodium heparin or K2EDTA tube instead.
  • Tacrolimus and cyclosporine are measured in whole blood (EDTA lavender top). Serum or plasma, or a tube already spun and separated, is the wrong specimen: recollect. Trough (C0) is the usual sample; cyclosporine may also be monitored at 2 h (C2). Immunoassay and LC-MS/MS results differ, so follow a patient on one method.
  • High-dose methotrexate: levels at set times after the infusion. Consensus thresholds for considering glucarpidase include above 30 µmol/L at 36 h, above 10 at 42 h, or above 5 at 48 h with a rising creatinine. Glucarpidase turns methotrexate into DAMPA, which most immunoassays also detect, so immunoassay overestimates methotrexate for about 48 h afterwards; a chromatographic method (HPLC) separates them.
Critical values vary by laboratory; these are one published set.

Toxicology in depth

StepWorked example: Na 138, glucose 90, BUN 14, ethanol 230 mg/dL, measured 366 mOsm/kg
2 × Na276
Glucose/18 + BUN/2.85 + 5 = 10
Ethanol/4.650
Calculated osmolality336 mOsm/kg
Gap366 − 336 = 30: ethanol does not explain it; request specific methanol and ethylene glycol levels
  • Leaving the ethanol term out gives a gap of 80 in this example and wrongly blames ethanol for all of it. The ethanol divisor differs between references (4.6 in this app, 4.25 or 3.7 elsewhere), so state the formula with the result.
  • Time course: early, the parent alcohol raises the osmolal gap and the anion gap may be normal; as it is metabolized to organic acids the anion gap rises and the osmolal gap falls. Neither gap is sensitive enough to rule a toxic alcohol out.
  • Measure osmolality by freezing point depression. A vapor pressure osmometer does not detect volatile solutes such as methanol and ethanol and can hide the gap.
  • Ethylene glycol: calcium oxalate crystals appear in urine 4-8 h after ingestion (envelope-shaped dihydrate first, needle-shaped monohydrate later); their absence does not exclude it. Glycolic acid resembles lactate and is read as lactate by lactate oxidase methods (blood gas and point-of-care analyzers) but not by lactate dehydrogenase methods: a 'lactate gap' between the two is a clue.
  • Other causes of an osmolal gap: osmotic agents such as mannitol, and ethanol metabolites (acetaldehyde, acetate), which can make ethanol raise the gap more than its own term predicts.
  • Enzymatic ethanol (alcohol dehydrogenase, NADH read at 340 nm) can read falsely positive when LD and lactate are both very high, as in acetaminophen liver necrosis, end-stage liver disease or hypoxic injury: LD turns lactate into pyruvate and makes NADH too. Recent analyzers are affected mainly above about LD 25,000 U/L with lactate over 14 mmol/L, or LD 40,000 U/L with lactate over 4 mmol/L, and the size of the effect depends on the kit (reported false values about 0.2-2.0 g/L, 20-200 mg/dL). An ethanol that does not fit the patient or the osmolal gap is confirmed by gas chromatography.
PoisonMechanism and pitfalls
AcetaminophenNAPQI from cytochrome P450 depletes glutathione and causes liver necrosis. The Rumack-Matthew nomogram applies only to a single acute ingestion with a known time, from 4 h on; levels before 4 h may not be the peak. Extended-release products: repeat 4 h later. Repeated ingestion: use AST, ALT and the level, not the nomogram. Enzymes are not reliable in the first 24 h and peak at 72-96 h.
SalicylateEarly respiratory alkalosis, then mixed, then metabolic acidosis with a raised anion gap. Absorption can continue (bezoars), so repeat levels until they fall. Therapeutic 10-20 mg/dL (0.72-1.45 mmol/L); dialysis is considered above 100 mg/dL (7.25 mmol/L) acute or 60 (4.35) chronic.
LeadDiagnosed by whole blood lead. A capillary (fingerstick) result at or above 3.5 µg/dL is confirmed by venous draw: within 3 months for 3.5-9, 1 month for 10-19, 2 weeks for 20-44, 48 h for 45 or more. Lead impairs hemoglobin synthesis; basophilic stippling and microcytic or normocytic anemia suggest it but are neither sensitive nor specific. Erythrocyte (zinc) protoporphyrin is often inaccurate and seldom used now.
Carbon monoxideCo-oximetry measures carboxyhemoglobin directly; venous blood is acceptable. Pulse oximeters cannot tell carboxyhemoglobin from oxyhemoglobin, and a blood gas analyzer that calculates saturation from pO2 reads normal. Headache from about 10-20%, confusion above 30%, coma above 60%. Half-life about 4.5 h on room air, 1.5 h on 100% oxygen.

Drug testing and specimen validity

  • Each class immunoassay is calibrated to one target drug; other members of the group are detected only to the extent they cross-react. A negative screen does not mean a drug the assay barely recognizes is absent, and a positive screen is presumptive until confirmed.
  • Clinical testing: confirm when the result will drive a major decision, when a specific drug must be identified, or when the screen is unexpected; discuss unexpected results with the laboratory or toxicologist. Patients prescribed oxycodone, buprenorphine or methadone need tests specific for those drugs.
  • Federal workplace testing: a specimen of at least 45 mL, temperature read within 4 minutes (32-38 °C), chain of custody documented on the Federal Custody and Control Form, split specimens, and confirmation by mass spectrometry. A temperature out of range means a second collection under direct observation.
